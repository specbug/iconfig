Body Text — Long-form reading

Typography is the craft of endowing human language with a durable visual form, and thus with an independent existence. Its heartbeat is a steady, even pulse — the rhythm of text on a page. When done well, the reader forgets about the type entirely and is absorbed in the content.

Good proportional sans-serif fonts walk a tightrope between neutrality and character. Too neutral, and the font is invisible in the worst way — indistinguishable from system defaults, generic and forgettable. Too characterful, and it becomes a distraction, calling attention to itself when it should be serving the words.

R Sans aims for the sweet spot: warm enough to feel intentional, quiet enough to disappear during sustained reading. The softened terminals on c, e, s, and t create a gentle, organic texture — the typographic equivalent of rounded corners on a well-designed product.

Numbers should feel at home in running text: the figures 0123456789 sit on the baseline with tabular or proportional spacing. At body sizes, the optical size axis ensures optimal stroke contrast and spacing for comfortable reading.

What makes R Sans different from stock DM Sans:

  • Terminal softening — 12 key glyphs have warmer, rounded stroke endings
  • Disambiguation — Serifed I, tailed l, dotted 0 (static instances)
  • Proper metadata — Clean RIBBI grouping, correct fsSelection/macStyle per weight
  • Cross-platform metrics — hhea aligned with usWin for consistent rendering

DM Sans advantages over the previous Inter base:

  1. Geometric DNA — no fighting neo-grotesque origins
  2. Optical size axis (9-40) for automatic text/display switching
  3. Same design team as Google Sans Text (Colophon Foundry)
  4. Body-text-optimized metrics (~1.30x default line-height)
  5. Weight range up to 1000 (Black) for variable font

The Invisible Art of Type Selection

By Rishit V. — 19 Feb 2026 — 4 min read

A well-chosen typeface is like good posture: you only notice it when it's wrong. But the difference between "fine" and "right" compounds over hours of reading.

Most developers never think about their UI font. They inherit whatever the OS provides — San Francisco on macOS, Segoe UI on Windows, Roboto on Android — and move on. And honestly? Those defaults are excellent. They're the product of millions of dollars and years of research.

But defaults are designed for everyone, which means they're optimized for no one in particular. A custom font lets you make opinionated choices: do you prefer geometric warmth or humanist flexibility? Tabular or proportional figures? Softened or clinical terminals?

Why DM Sans as the Foundation

DM Sans was designed by Colophon Foundry — the same team behind Google Sans Text. It has geometric DNA from birth: you don't have to fight neo-grotesque origins to make it feel warm and round. The optical size axis (9-40) automatically adjusts stroke contrast and spacing for different sizes.

At text sizes (opsz=9-20), DM Sans uses double-storey a and g — better for body text readability. At display sizes (opsz=20-40), it switches to the more geometric single-storey forms.

Terminal Softening

The core creative transformation is terminal softening. DM Sans has clean, precise flat-cut terminals — beautiful for a geometric font, but slightly clinical for sustained reading. R Sans pulls terminal tips inward by 5-18 units (on a 1000 UPM grid) across 12 key glyphs.

The effect is subtle on any individual letter but cumulative across a paragraph. Compare the c, e, and s in the A/B comparison above — the rounder terminals give R Sans a gentle warmth that stock DM Sans lacks.
